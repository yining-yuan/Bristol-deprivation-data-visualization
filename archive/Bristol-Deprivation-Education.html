A tale of two classrooms

Bristol is the most highly qualified core city in the UK, but it is also a place where extreme educational inequality persists

Stark within-city disparities can be seen in the number of adults achieving Level 4+ qualifications in Bristol's lowest (Redland) and highest (Hartcliffe and Withywood) areas of deprivation.

Some of the city's most deprived areas are home to the highest proportions of children. Growing up in low-income households and deprived areas affects young people's access to opportunities.

The attainment journey

Educational outcomes in Bristol align with national averages at the start of school but soon fall behind

Pupils in Reception slightly exceed national averages for reaching a 'good level of development'. Yet by the end of key stage 2, the trend reverses and a marginally lower percentage of pupils (60%) than the national average (63%) achieve the 'expected' standard in reading, writing and maths.
