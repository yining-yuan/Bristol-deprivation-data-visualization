A tale of two cities

Bristol is prosperous on paper, and unequal in practice.

It has the highest share of degree-educated adults of any English core city. It also contains some of the most deprived neighbourhoods in the country, often a few miles apart.

This story follows that divide through a single life course — tracing how an early gap is rarely closed, and usually grows.

Ages 0–5 · Early years

Before school even starts, the gap is already open.

More than a third of children arrive at reception not "school ready" — and the children least ready are concentrated in the city's poorest wards. The starting line is not the same for everyone.

Ages 5–11 · Primary

The gap widens — in the classroom and the body.

Across primary school, two trends run together: attainment slips below the national standard, and children become markedly less active. Childhood obesity nearly doubles between reception and Year 6.

Ages 16–25 · Transition to adulthood

The cliff edge between education and work.

The journey to adulthood is getting longer and more precarious. Routes into work are thin, ill health among young adults is rising sharply, and many cannot afford to leave home. For care leavers, who are over-represented in Bristol, the supports fall away earliest.

Three charts, three pressures: too few routes in, rising ill health, and a delayed, unaffordable independence.

Sources & how this updates

One data file behind every number.

Every figure on this page is read from a single file, bristol-data.js. To refresh the story when a source publishes new data, update the value and date there — no other code changes.

There is no single API across these publishers, so figures are maintained centrally and each links to its public source for verification. For an automated feed, host a JSON of the same shape and set liveUrl — the page will pull and merge it on load.
